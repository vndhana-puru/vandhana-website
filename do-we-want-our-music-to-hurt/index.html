Modern media consumption is oriented around the escape of discomfort.

Short form content dominates. Literacy rates have plummeted. News feeds cater to opinions instead of challenging them.

So — where do we turn when the road runs out? When circumventing reality turns into a spiral, and the emotion at the crux of the issue is evidently not going anywhere?

There are few areas in life where people willingly seek out pain. But music is one of them.

Which begs the question — when we go to the ends of the earth to avoid pain, do we want our music to hurt?

To find out, I scraped the lyrics for all ~7,000 songs on the 500 Greatest Albums of All Time and deployed a language model to classify each by its relationship to pain.

Inspired by philosopher Kieran Setiya's distinction between atelic experiences (valued in the doing) and telic ones (aimed at an endpoint), I sorted each song by whether it sits with pain, moves past it, or stands outside it.
